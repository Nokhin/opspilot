# Business Continuity Procedure

## 1. Continuity assessment
The Operations Duty Manager assesses continuity when a disruption prevents essential customer services and routine recovery is unlikely to restore them promptly. Identify service priority, affected sites, dependencies and safe alternatives. Continuity maintains essential service while recovery addresses the failed component; these activities can run together. A workaround must be approved by its operational owner and must not bypass evidence or payment controls. The assessment is recorded in the incident timeline with known facts and assumptions.

## 2. Activation thresholds
Activate the continuity assessment immediately for an unsafe site or a multi-site essential-service closure. For a customer-facing digital or payment outage lasting 60 minutes or more, the Duty Manager must decide whether to activate the approved continuity plan and record the decision and rationale. The 60-minute threshold is an assessment deadline, not permission to delay SEV1 escalation at 30 minutes. POL-INC-001 and POL-INC-002 continue to apply during continuity. Safety takes priority over maintaining transaction throughput.

## 3. Alternative service capacity
Before redirecting customers, the receiving-site owner confirms safe capacity, accessibility, staffing and transaction controls. The contact centre confirms the customer instructions and expected waiting conditions. Do not assume every site can perform every service. For digital interruption, use only an approved alternative workflow with reconciliation controls; do not collect payment credentials in spreadsheets or messaging channels. Record the alternative's owner and limits. POL-FAC-001 governs facility safety and POL-PAY-001 governs payment-integrity restrictions.

## 4. Coordination and updates
Operations owns the continuity decision; Facilities confirms physical safety; Digital Services confirms technical recovery; Customer Service manages customer messages. Risk & Compliance advises on data and transaction controls. Assign a named coordinator and checkpoint for the alternate operation. Continue SEV1 or SEV2 update cadence under POL-COM-001. Supplier delays must be recorded under POL-VEN-001, but supplier accountability does not replace HarbourLink's customer-service ownership. Record why a proposed alternate was rejected if it cannot operate safely.

## 5. Return to normal operation
Return to normal operation only after the recovery owner confirms stability and the alternative-service owner reconciles outstanding work. The Duty Manager approves the return, identifies any customers requiring follow-up and ensures both sites and the contact centre use consistent instructions. Avoid switching back solely because one test transaction succeeds. Maintain a rollback option until observation under POL-REC-001 is complete. An assistant can explain the decision criteria but cannot activate or deactivate the continuity plan.

## 6. Exercises and learning
Operations schedules a continuity exercise every six months and records gaps, owners and due dates. A real activation is included in the post-incident review under POL-REV-001. Preserve decisions, capacity checks, customer notices and return-to-service evidence under POL-EVD-001. This fictional procedure contains no regulatory reporting deadlines or promises about physical emergency response times. Where a required site-specific safety instruction is absent, obtain the approved site plan; do not replace missing internal evidence with a general web answer.
